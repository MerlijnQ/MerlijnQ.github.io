Mind Wandering (MW) decreases performance in various at
tention tasks. We investigate how a task-relevant (cue) and a
task-irrelevant (distraction) auditory stimulus affect MW in a
sustained attention response task (SART). Using the ACT-R ar
chitecture, we build a simple MW model in which we simulate
three conditions, consisting of a condition without an auditory
stimulus and the different stimuli previously stated. Although
MWremained near 50% across conditions, our results reveal
that the auditory stimuli affects the timing of MW. Cues sig
nificantly reduced MW at moments when a button press was
required in response to a visual stimulus. The task-irrelevant
condition displayed a similar but less pronounced effect. A
one-way ANOVA confirmed significant differences between
the conditions in MW frequency upon the visual stimulus on
set in the SART, indicating auditory stimuli increase attention
when needed in a task. Therefore, we cannot conclude that the
auditory stimuli influences the total time spent MW, but it does
influence when you mind wander.
